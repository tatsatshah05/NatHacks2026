# OP-417: Walk and Bus Trips

Purpose: let a walk and transit trip ride one ETS bus, reusing the OP-311 machinery. Story: [issue #188](https://github.com/tatsatshah05/NatHacks2026/issues/188). Lead decision, November 8 evening, after user research asked for buses.

## Turning it on

`TRANSIT_BUS` (`true`, `1`, `on` or `yes`; off by default, `sync: false` in `render.yaml`) extends `LRT_TRIPS`: with both on, `GET /api/route?mode=walk_lrt` considers walk, one LRT ride (with one LRT transfer, as before), walk and walk, one bus ride, walk, and the fastest trip that beats walking comes first. `TRANSIT_BUS` alone does nothing. With it off, the walk and LRT response is today's plus the two additive fields below (`cloud/tests/test_bus.py::test_flag_off_equals_the_response_recorded_before_this_story` compares with the response recorded before this story). The query parameter and the existing fields keep their names: a bus trip still arrives in `legs` and `lrt`.

Locally: `LRT_TRIPS=true TRANSIT_BUS=true make dev`, then for example
`curl "localhost:8000/api/route?from=53.5527,-113.5445&to=53.5610,-113.5000&profile=wheelchair&mode=walk_lrt"`.

## Choosing transit (OP-420)

`GET /api/route?mode=walk_lrt` takes an optional `transit` parameter (additive; approved by the lead on November 9; [issue #199](https://github.com/tatsatshah05/NatHacks2026/issues/199)). `faster`, the default, is the behaviour above: a bus or LRT trip appears only when it beats the best walking route, and the response is byte for byte what it was without the parameter. `always` includes the planner's best trip even when it is slower than walking: it is ranked after the walking routes that beat it, the slowest walking route is dropped so there are still at most three routes, and its reason says by how much ("Walk and bus: about 6 min slower than walking; shown because you asked for transit", or "about the same time as walking; shown because you asked for transit" when the difference rounds to zero minutes; a trip that is faster keeps its usual reason). `only` returns transit trips alone, up to three distinct ones fastest first (boarding the same line at another stop is the same trip at a worse time, not an alternative); when no trip exists at all the walking routes come back, `mode` stays `walk_lrt`, and the warnings carry "No bus or LRT trip was found for this journey; these are walking routes." followed by the planner's reason (no stop in reach, nothing running at this hour, or a station this profile cannot use). The parameter is read only with `mode=walk_lrt` and a loaded planner, and ignored otherwise; an unknown value is a 422; nothing was added to the response. In `cloud/app/lrt.py` the search (`LrtPlanner._search`, every option sorted by time, LRT before a bus that ties) is separate from the choice (`plan`, which applies the policy and fills `LrtPlan.trips`), so trips are found and described the same way under every policy. Tests: `cloud/tests/test_transit_choice.py`. The web control for this parameter is not in yet.

## Files

- `cloud/app/lrt_gtfs.py`: `read_bus` (one line per stop pattern) beside `read_lrt`; the stop-time and headway helpers are shared, and `lrt.json` rebuilds byte for byte apart from `built_at` (checked on the feed of November 8, 2026).
- `scripts/build_bus_graph.py` (`make bus`): writes `cloud/data/bus.json`, format 2, compact, one stop and one line per text line.
- `cloud/app/lrt.py`: `LrtSchedule.load(path, bus_path)` puts bus stops in the station table (`vehicle` "bus", no devices, `at_grade`) and bus lines in the line list (`one_way`); `LrtPlanner` indexes which lines call where, pairs candidate stops per line and plans both vehicles; `station_access` is open for a bus stop.
- `cloud/app/main.py`: loads `bus.json` when both flags are on; a bad `bus.json` logs and leaves the LRT alone.
- `web/`: "Include LRT and bus trips"; a bus ride renders like an LRT ride ("Bus 2 toward Stadium", stops as rings labelled "Bus stop"); the map legend says what the double line is.

## How a bus trip is planned

1. The two Dijkstra searches of OP-311 (from the start and from the end, 1.2 km of walking cost) now give the walk to every LRT station and every bus stop in reach, in one vectorised pass over the entry nodes found at start-up.
2. LRT pairs are evaluated exactly as before. For buses, every line with a stop in reach of both ends is considered; for each such line the nearest `BUS_STOPS_PER_LINE` (3) stops by walking cost at each end are paired, and a pair's ride combines every line serving both stops (the patterns of one route add their frequencies, as Capital and Metro do on their shared track). A bus line is ridden forward only; a loop route's repeated stop takes the shortest ride forward.
3. Cost is time, as for the LRT: walk + 30 s at the stop (the `at_grade` assumption) + half the headway + the scheduled ride + 30 s + walk. The cheapest trip of either vehicle wins when it beats walking; an exact tie goes to the LRT, which is evaluated first.
4. Text: "Go to 130 Street & 111 Avenue (stop 1658). Sidewalk conditions at this stop come from the walking legs.", then "Take bus 3 toward Stadium or bus 111 toward Concordia Univ from ... to ...: 11 stops, about 20 min scheduled; low-floor bus. Wait about 5 min (estimate from the schedule, not a live arrival).", then "Get off at ...". The reason reads "Walk and bus: about N min faster than walking". The unmapped-elevator warning is not added to a bus trip: a stop is where the schedule puts it.

## Response fields (additive, only with `mode=walk_lrt`)

- `legs[].vehicle`: `"lrt"` or `"bus"` on ride legs, `null` on walks.
- `legs[].route_short_names`: one per entry of `lines`, the number or name on the vehicle (`["Capital"]`, `["3", "111"]`), `null` on walks.
- Unchanged fields now also describe buses: `lines` is the route's long name, `headsigns` the destination without the route number ETS repeats in front of it, `lrt.stations` includes the stops used (`access` "at_grade", no devices, `note` the bus-stop sentence), `warnings` says "between buses".

## Assumptions and limits

| Name | Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `BUS_STOPS_PER_LINE` | 3 | Candidate stops per bus line at each end, nearest by walking cost (a tuning constant, not measured) |
| Access at a bus stop | 30 s each end | The existing `at_grade` assumption |
| Transfers | none | Walk, one ride, walk in version 1 |
| Headways | median gap at the first stop, busiest date per day type | A pattern with one trip a day has no gap, so no headway |

ETS buses are low-floor (the lead's statement in issue #188); every ETS bus trip in the feed carries GTFS `wheelchair_accessible` 1 (measured). The stop itself is a point on the sidewalk, so its conditions are those of the walking legs, which the trip text says.

## Measured (November 8, 2026, a MacBook, the committed network and data)

- `bus.json`: 182 ETS routes, 527 lines (stop patterns), 5,273 stops, 0.98 MB, built in 9.3 s. Loading it with `lrt.json`: 40 ms; `LrtPlanner` start-up with 5,303 stations: 60 ms; `load_planner` total 101 ms (target 150 ms). LRT only: 1 ms.
- Planning one trip (the planner alone, after the walking route): 1 to 2 ms with buses, 1 to 2 ms without, on seven real pairs.
- Westmount Centre (53.5527, -113.5445) to Kingsway Mall (53.5610, -113.5000) by wheelchair on a Tuesday at 11:00: a 33.6 min trip (bus 3 toward Stadium or bus 111 toward Concordia Univ or Stadium, 11 stops, 5.0 min estimated wait) against 77.8 min walking; no LRT trip exists there. Bonnie Doon to Churchill Square by wheelchair: 31.3 min by the 500X express against 39.3 min by the LRT and 102.1 min walking.
- `GET /api/route` latency: see the PR body (measured with `scripts/route_bench.py` against a local instance with and without `TRANSIT_BUS`).
