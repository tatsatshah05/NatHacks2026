# OP-205: Camera and weather ice risk

For [issue #48](https://github.com/tatsatshah05/NatHacks2026/issues/48), targeting
`phase_2`. `cloud/app/camera_ice.py` evaluates confirmed camera evidence and
OP-109's weather watch, then matches local ice risk to OP-104 crossing edges.
It creates existing shared `Obstruction` records at medium confidence.

**Wired into the app.** `cloud/app/main.py` registers `CameraIceHazardProvider`
(`camera_ice_routing.py`) as `app.state.hazard_provider`. It reads OP-213's camera
store and OP-109's weather service, wraps OP-206's matcher, and `GET /api/route`
avoids or costs the iced crossings per profile and lists each camera risk with its
full record in the OP-208 report. This branch changes no shared schema, endpoint,
configuration or routing weights. Real trained-model and staging acceptance remain
pending; the tests use synthetic readings only.

## Rules

All camera evidence must be unexpired, within the shared five-second clock-skew tolerance, and in the application's
live/replay mode. Pass only confirmed OP-204 outputs, after its model confidence
gate and two-observation stability filter; raw model guesses are not inputs.

| Camera evidence | Weather evidence | Local ice risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ice` | Any, including unavailable | Yes, at medium confidence |
| `wet` | Fresh active watch, freezing air or ground | Yes, at medium confidence |
| `wet` | Inactive watch | No |
| `wet` | Both air and ground above zero, even if active flag is inconsistent | No; diagnostic for inconsistency |
| `wet` | Missing, stale, future or wrong-mode watch | Uncertain; no new hazard or clear instruction |
| `dry`, `snow_partial`, `snow_covered` | Any | No ice-risk record; snow handling remains separate |
| `unknown` | Any | Uncertain; no new hazard or clear instruction |

Zero degrees counts as freezing. An active watch still comes from OP-109's full
moisture/melt/frost rule; freezing temperature by itself is insufficient for a
wet reading. A city-wide watch without a wet/ice camera never creates a local
routing hazard. Wording is always "ice risk", never a claim of detected black ice.

## Inputs, freshness and recovery

`CameraEvidence` is a local validated projection of OP-204's accepted reading,
not a new shared contract. It accepts worker objects, actual camera-store rows or their dictionaries and
reads `source`, `source_ref`, `label`, `latitude`, `longitude`, `observed_at`,
`expires_at`, `mode` and `attribution`. Additional model/view fields are ignored. The exact
ingest shape is accepted in OP-213; it is merged, with deployment verification pending. Times must include a timezone;
nonfinite/out-of-range coordinates, invalid lifetimes and blank IDs are rejected.
One malformed row cannot discard valid neighbours.

Use `match_service_camera_ice()` with the real `WeatherService` where possible.
It passes the retained watch and calculates freshness from `last_success` age
only (`weather_is_stale()`): missing or more than three refresh intervals
(30 minutes) old is stale. A refresh error alone does not invalidate the cache.
The age is measured on the service's own clock, which stamped `last_success`; in
replay mode that clock is frozen at the recording. For
isolated callers, `match_camera_ice()` accepts `WeatherResponse` plus
`weather_stale`; the public weather response has no stale field. Wet readings
also enforce OP-109's existing two-hour provider observation limit. A recent
cached active watch remains usable after a failed refresh; its observation must
still be within two hours. Camera and weather clocks allow five seconds ahead.
The temperature check is a consistency guard for malformed cached watch data.

The result preserves the camera's observation time. Ice-only expiry is the
camera expiry; wet-plus-watch expiry is the earlier of camera expiry and the
watch observation's age limit. Re-evaluating weather never makes old camera
evidence fresh. Wet risk attributes 511 Alberta, Environment and Climate Change
Canada, and Open-Meteo; ice-only risk preserves the upstream camera attribution. Both use category
`weather`. If the full combined credit would exceed the shared 200-character
limit, camera attribution stays intact and weather attribution goes in detail.

Keep the newest reading per source reference. Identical duplicates collapse;
contradictory records with the same timestamp are uncertain in either order.
Newer dry evidence supersedes older ice evidence. Source references remain
stable across refreshes: obstruction IDs are `camera_model:<camera_ref>:ice_risk`.

## Crossing matching and routing handoff

Build `CrossingIndex(network)` once at startup, rebuilding when the network
changes. It selects only `EDGE_CROSSING` edges using OP-104's flag constant and
indexes every polyline segment. Per-camera distance calculations are vectorised
in NumPy using a local metre projection. No geocoder, external request or graph
rebuild occurs at route time. Each index caches up to 512 camera positions
(including empty matches); unchanged coordinates reuse the result, moved cameras
are recomputed, and the oldest position is evicted when the cache is full.

The radius is **50 m inclusive**, measured to the nearest point on the full
polyline, including bends and segment interiors. An adjacent ordinary sidewalk
is never selected merely because it is close to the camera. Geometry uses the
network's stored float32 coordinates, so boundary accuracy is limited by that
data; no extra geographic buffer is added. An unlocated/malformed crossing is a
network-data problem, not permission to match unrelated paths.

`CameraIceResult` contains:

- `mode`: explicit live/replay provenance, which must match the caller's app.
- `risks`: shared obstruction records paired with exact `edge_indices`.
- `by_edge()`: edge index to obstruction tuple, ready for the matcher/router to
  inspect without changing contracts. Overlapping cameras retain separate evidence;
  the router applies its existing strongest-effect rule, not multiplied risks.
- `hazards()`: the existing `HazardMap` of exact crossing indices to
  `EdgeHazard` tuples, directly accepted by `profile_weights()` and the router.
- `warnings`: unavailable evidence, malformed/conflicting rows or no crossing
  within 50 m. An unmatched camera must not affect arbitrary nearby footways.
- `clear_refs`: only camera references with current affirmative non-risk evidence.
- `uncertain_refs`: known references whose current state cannot be established.

The Point geometry identifies the camera, not a generic obstruction-match radius.
**Use the supplied crossing indices; never feed these points into the generic
point matcher**, which could tag ordinary sidewalks or miss crossings 30–50 m
away, and whose area rule would make every `weather` item city-wide. The matcher
therefore leaves any `camera_model` item alone (`matching.CAMERA_SOURCE`), and
camera risks reach routing only through `CameraIceHazardProvider`. Existing blocked
barriers, profile costs, maximum-overlap rules and city-wide caution remain
unchanged.

This helper does not upsert or delete anything. The ingest/store integration
must only clear the corresponding local ice record for an explicit `clear_refs`
entry. Missing rows, warnings and empty `risks` do not instruct bulk deletion.
Keep previous uncertain records only until their original expiry; do not renew
them or present them as confirmed clear. The weather watch remains a separate
city-wide advisory. No source or state is persisted by this helper.

## Stored readings to routing

`main.py` builds the provider once at startup, beside the `Router`, after the
weather service exists:

```python
app.state.hazard_provider = CameraIceHazardProvider(
    network,  # the Router's network; builds the CrossingIndex
    camera_store.latest,  # OP-213 rows, unexpired at the request time
    weather=app.state.weather_service,  # None with SCHEDULER=off
    base=app.state.hazard_provider,  # OP-206's HazardProvider
    mode=app.state.mode,
)
```

If the crossing index cannot be built, startup logs it and routing keeps the
matcher alone. With `SCHEDULER=off` there is no weather service: `ice` readings
still count and `wet` readings are uncertain.

On each route request the provider:

- reads the camera store (one small query) and the cached watch;
- evaluates the rule again only when a reading, the watch or its staleness
  changed, a risk reached its expiry, or 10 s (`RECHECK_S`) passed, so
  time-dependent checks catch up; crossing matches are cached per camera position;
- merges the camera hazards with the matcher's map only when either side changed,
  returning the same map object otherwise.

On the city network the crossing index builds in about 14 ms, the first
evaluation of 20 new camera positions takes about 30 ms, and a later call takes
about 0.5 ms, most of it the SQLite read (measured locally on a laptop, not on
Render).

Failure handling: a failed camera read keeps the unexpired risks and logs the
error type only (database errors can carry connection details); a failed matcher
keeps its last good map; any other error in the provider is logged and the
matcher's map is returned unchanged. `/api/route` never fails because of cameras.

The provider retains uncertain or missing camera risks only until their original
expiry, clears only current affirmative non-risk evidence, and keeps other
barriers intact. It copies the matcher's lists. A camera `ice_risk` that reached
the matcher as a generic point is removed from that map, so only the exact
crossing matches count; blocked barriers remain. OP-207 applies the profile
effects (walker or cane avoids the crossing, wheelchair and low vision pay ×3,
standard ×1.5) and lists the camera item in `hazards_on_route` or
`hazards_avoided`.

`provider.latest` holds the current risks with their full `Obstruction` records
and diagnostics; `provider.items` indexes those records, and
`routing_api.get_report_items` passes that index to the OP-208 report before the
matcher's own, so camera items are listed under Weather and ice with source,
attribution, detail and `observed_at`. Retained records live in memory and reset
at restart; an outage never renews them. The provider performs no store writes or
external requests, so camera risks do not appear on `GET /api/obstructions` yet.

### Replay mode

The ingest API accepts only readings in the API's own mode. Camera times are
checked against the app's time. Without `REPLAY_AT` that is the real clock, so a
replay worker posting current timestamps works for `ice`; `wet` readings stay
uncertain because the recorded weather observation is more than two hours old. If
`REPLAY_AT` freezes the app's clock (PR #136), replay readings must be stamped on
that clock too, or they are treated as future-dated and ignored.

From the repository root after installing the repository requirements:

```bash
python -m pytest cloud/tests/test_camera_ice.py -q
python -m pytest cloud/tests/test_camera_ice_routing.py cloud/tests/test_camera_ice_review.py -q
python -m pytest cloud/tests/test_camera_ice_app.py -q
python -m pytest -q

```

Tests use synthetic readings/networks and the committed city network. No camera
frames, trained model, API key or external HTTP is required. They cover every
rule branch, source failure/recovery, mode and freshness boundaries, 49/50/51 m
distances, curved crossings, ordinary-sidewalk exclusion and unchanged graph data.
`test_camera_ice_app.py` posts readings through `POST /api/ingest/camera` into the
real store and checks `/api/route` and its report: walker or cane detours, a
wheelchair keeps the crossing at ×3, failures leave routing working, and
`create_app()` wires the provider on the city network.

### Combined local pipeline check

In a local checkout containing OP-204, OP-205, OP-206 and OP-213, with the worker
test dependencies installed:

```bash
python scripts/check_camera_pipeline.py
```

This is a repeatable synthetic check, not a live-camera demo. It generates a tiny
ONNX model and two different images in memory, runs the real worker, POSTs through
the real API to in-memory SQLite, reads the actual camera store and calls the real
route API through OP-206 and `CameraIceHazardProvider`. The HTTP camera transport,
credentials and weather are synthetic. It verifies four profile routes/reports,
wrong-key rejection, weather/camera outage retention and warm-weather recovery.

The verified test route is 200 m before ice risk, 280 m when the crossing is
avoided, and 200 m after recovery. It makes zero external requests and saves zero
images. These are synthetic test distances, not a measured Edmonton journey or
trained-model accuracy result. The separate branches were combined only in an
ignored local integration worktree; no protected branch or deployment was changed.

## Review decision: camera-specific matching

Prefer a source-specific `camera_model` path using `CrossingIndex`. The provider
implements it without changing OP-206's general rules or the shared obstruction
schema; the matcher only skips `camera_model` items so they can never become
city-wide areas. Keep the Point at the real camera for provenance and use the
exact crossing indices for effects. Converting those edges to a MultiLineString
would introduce a second geometry representation and generic buffering could
affect neighbouring sidewalks.
