# OP-311: Walk and LRT Trips

Purpose: plan trips that combine walking and the LRT, and use only stations whose elevators work for people who cannot use stairs. Story: [issue #66](https://github.com/tatsatshah05/NatHacks2026/issues/66).

## Turning it on

The feature is behind `LRT_TRIPS` (`true`, `1`, `on` or `yes`; off by default, and `"false"` in `render.yaml`).

- **Off, or `mode=walk`:** `GET /api/route` is exactly the walking planner. The response is byte-for-byte the same as before this story (tested, and checked on 36 recorded requests).
- **On, with `mode=walk_lrt`:** when the best walk and LRT trip is faster than the best walking route, it comes first (rank 1, reason "Walk and LRT: about N min faster than walking"), and the slowest walking route is dropped so the response still has at most 3 routes, best first (PLAN.md section 8, step 5). The walking routes that stay keep their order and move down one rank. When no trip qualifies, the walking routes are unchanged. Either way each walking route says `"mode": "walk"`, the response says `"mode": "walk_lrt"`, and its `warnings` explain when no trip is offered (a station not usable for this profile, no train at this hour, walking faster).
- Any other `mode` is a 422.

Locally: `LRT_TRIPS=true make dev`, then for example
`curl "localhost:8000/api/route?from=53.5444,-113.4909&to=53.4853,-113.5169&profile=wheelchair&mode=walk_lrt"`.

## Files

- `cloud/app/lrt_gtfs.py`: reads a GTFS zip into stations, line order, ride times, headways and transfer pairs (build time only).
- `scripts/build_lrt_graph.py` (`make lrt`): one official download, adds the City device list and OP-106 anchors, writes `cloud/data/lrt.json`.
- `cloud/app/lrt.py`: loads `lrt.json`, applies the elevator rule, plans the trip, builds its report and the response models.
- `cloud/app/routing_api.py`: the `mode` query parameter and the optional `mode`, `legs` and `lrt` fields. `cloud/app/main.py` loads the planner at startup when the flag is on.
- `cloud/tests/test_lrt.py`: a synthetic GTFS zip, a synthetic street and schedule, API tests and one test on the real network.

## Which stations a profile may use

Profiles that cannot use stairs (`stairs: null` in `config/profiles.json`: wheelchair, and walker or cane) enter and leave stations only through working, mapped elevators:

1. An `at_grade` station (no City elevator and no GTFS elevator pathway) needs no elevator.
2. Every `platform` elevator must be in service. When a station has `entrance` elevators, at least one must be in service, and the trip names the working ones ("Use an entrance with a working elevator: Winspear, Churchill Connector.").
3. A station whose elevators OpenPath cannot check is not used: no elevator in the device list, an elevator outage for a device it cannot place, or no device status at all.
4. A transfer station must be usable too.

Standard and low vision profiles may use every station. Escalator outages never close a station; they are listed in the report for everyone.

"Mapped" means the elevator is tied to its station by City device id (all 32 City elevators are), so its live status is known. None of the 32 has a reviewed OpenStreetMap match yet (OP-106), so the walk ends at the station's GTFS location and every trip says so. When OP-106 reviews a match, its anchor node becomes the station's entry node on the next `make lrt`.

**Live status** is the store's `elevator_out` and `escalator_out` items from the City outage feed (`city_lrt_devices`, OP-105). An item belongs to a station by `source_ref` (the device id) or, for a device missing from the list, by the station name at the start of `detail` ("Southgate, East Entrance"), through the explicit aliases in `cloud/app/lrt_gtfs.py`. When the scheduler marks the source stale, the trip warns that elevator status was last confirmed at a given time.

## How a trip is built

1. The walking planner snaps the start and end as usual. The LRT planner reuses those nodes.
2. Two Dijkstra searches on the profile's weights, hazards included (from the start, and from the end), limited to `MAX_WALK_M` (1.2 km of walking cost), give the walk to every station within reach. A station joins the network at its entry nodes: the reviewed OP-106 anchors, or else the nodes within 200 m of its location, found once at startup.
3. For each usable entry and exit station: a direct ride when a line serves both, or else one transfer (at a shared station, or between a GTFS transfer pair such as Churchill Station and the Valley Line's Churchill Stop).
4. Cost is time: walk time (length / speed x multiplier, as routing computes it) + station access + estimated wait + scheduled ride (+ transfer) + station access + walk time. The cheapest trip wins and is returned only when it is faster than the best walking route; it then becomes rank 1 and replaces the slowest walking route.
5. The report covers the two walks like any route (on them, within 50 m, avoided). Every elevator and escalator outage at a station the trip uses is listed on the trip, under Indoor. Outages that made a nearby station unusable are listed as avoided, with the reason.

## The wait estimate

The wait is half the scheduled headway: the median gap between departures for the hour the rider boards, on the matching day type (weekday, Saturday or Sunday). Between midnight and 03:00 the previous day's schedule applies, as GTFS writes those trips as times past 24:00. Lines sharing a track (Capital and Metro between Health Sciences and Churchill) combine their frequencies. The response always labels it: `legs[].wait.estimate` is `true`, `wait.note` says it is not a live arrival, the ride step says "estimate from the schedule", and the trip's `warnings` say so again.

## Assumptions (not measured)

| Name | Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ACCESS_S` | 120 s with elevators, 30 s at grade | Street to platform, each end |
| `TRANSFER_PENALTY_S` | 180 s | Changing trains inside one station |
| `MAX_WALK_M` | 1,200 | Walking cost limit to and from a station |
| `ENTRY_RADIUS_M` | 200 m | Network nodes this close to a station can be its entry |
| At-grade stations | 16 stations | Reachable without stairs, inferred from the device list and GTFS pathways |

## Response fields (optional, only with `mode=walk_lrt`)

- `RouteResponse.mode`: `"walk_lrt"`.
- `RouteOut.mode`: `"walk"` or `"walk_lrt"`. A walk and LRT trip is always `routes[0]`.
- `RouteOut.legs`: `walk`, `ride`, `transfer`, `ride`, `walk`. Each has names, distance, duration and a GeoJSON line. A ride has `lines`, `line_colors`, `headsigns`, `stops`, `ride_min` and `wait` (`minutes`, `estimate: true`, `headway_min`, `note`).
- `RouteOut.lrt`: the stations used, each with `access`, `usable`, `note` and every elevator and escalator with `in_service` or `out_of_service`; plus `walk_distance_m`, `wait_is_estimate: true`, the schedule version, the attribution and a Maps note.
- The trip's Maps links are transit links (`travelmode=transit` for Google, `dirflg=r` for Apple). Those apps plan their own transit trip and may not use the same stations; `lrt.maps_note` says so. This is a decision for the lead to confirm, since AGENTS.md documents walking links only.

`/api/route` now uses `response_model_exclude_unset=True`, so any future field with a default must be set explicitly, or it disappears from responses. That is what keeps walking responses byte-for-byte unchanged while the new fields stay optional (Pydantic's per-field `exclude_if` needs 2.11, and `cloud/requirements.txt` allows 2.9).

The web app does not show trips yet. Run `make types` when it starts using these fields.

## Measured

- Churchill to Southgate by wheelchair, replay data with `REPLAY_AT=2026-11-08T16:00:00Z` (a Sunday, 15 minute headway): a 33.1 min trip (3.0 min walk, 2 min access, 7.5 min estimated wait, 18 min Capital Line ride, 2 min access, 0.6 min walk), against 167.2 min for the best walking route. The response is the trip (rank 1), then the walking routes of 167.2 and 174.2 min; the 191.6 min walking route is dropped.
- Latency on a laptop, 40 requests each (4 trips x 2 profiles x 5, `SCHEDULER=off`): walk median 78 ms and p95 102 ms; walk_lrt median 70 ms and p95 106 ms.
